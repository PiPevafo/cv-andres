# {frontmatter.title}

**Areas of interest:**

- **Quantum simulation and tensor-network methods**
- **Algorithmic complexity and resource estimation in quantum circuits**
- **Quantum optimization and hybrid quantum-classical computing**

At Universidad de los Andes my current topic is quantum magic (nonstabilizerness) and its characterization through majorization. The goal of my master's project, currently at the thesis-proposal stage, is to use majorization to classify, measure or separate entangled magic states, for instance through minimization over Clifford orbits. More broadly, I work on quantum information and many-body physics from a computational and resource-theoretic perspective. The common thread is to understand how much entanglement, and how much non-stabilizer resource, a given quantum circuit architecture can generate, and to exploit that structure to simulate or bound it classically.

The starting point of my undergraduate thesis is the minimal-cut principle: for a circuit represented as a tensor network of qubit (dimension-2) bonds, the entanglement entropy of a bipartition $A|\bar A$ is bounded by the size of the minimal cut $\gamma_A^{\min}$ separating the two regions,

<div style={{textAlign: 'center'}}>
$$S_A \le |\gamma_A^{\min}|\,\ln 2 .$$
</div>

Building an efficient graph-based algorithm on this bound allowed me to study architecture-dependent saturation of the entanglement entropy and to establish a system-size-dependent entanglement velocity $v_E \sim 1/2L$, confirmed analytically and numerically with KPZ scaling. In my master's I am complementing this upper bound with a lower bound from the averaged purity, to sandwich the entanglement velocity of circuits doped with native three-qubit non-Clifford gates and to connect it with their nonstabilizerness.
